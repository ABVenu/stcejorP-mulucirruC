# What Masai can do


Masai has no full-time batch and no residential campus. Every student is part-time. Online assignment scores are a weak filter, because a finished file is easy to copy. This note is the club Masai can run anyway.

## Verdict

Divide the batch into two tracks. That split is the right design. Entry-level students and experienced people do not want the same thing, so one club with one perk will miss both.

| Track | Who | What they actually want | Perk Masai can promise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Students | Early career, here for a job | A job | A real interview or partner shortlist, already booked |
| Experienced | Working, or with real work behind them | Mentors, a network, a shot at promotion. Investment only if a cheque is actually in the room | A technical pitch and a summit with named people |

Run both tracks as a short hackathon on a real problem. Select on how far they got, defended live. Do not select on the assignment marks already in the course.

Three changes keep this from repeating Scaler’s Luminaries Club:

1. Do not promise a placement. Promise an interview that is already on a partner’s calendar. Masai’s own placement-pledge rule is the same line: interview access, not “you will get placed.”
2. Do not promise investment. Experienced people get the pitch and the summit. An investor is invited only after they have agreed to attend. The poster does not say “funding.”
3. Do not judge the hackathon from the file alone. The score is a live defense. A copied or generated submission fails when the judge changes one requirement and the person has to fix it on the call.

## Why two tracks

Scaler could put every fellow in one room because they were all full-time undergraduates on one campus, on the same clock. Masai cannot. An entry-level student wants a job. An experienced person already has a job, or has shipped real work, and will not spend evenings on a placement drill.

Antler, Z Fellows, the Thiel Fellowship, and Campus Fund never mixed these. They also never used course marks. They looked at something the person had already built. Masai’s version of that look is the hackathon, because most part-time students do not arrive with a company.

## The hackathon

One window. Two problem statements. Same evenings the batch already uses, so this replaces class for that stretch instead of adding a night. Experienced people will not do a 48-hour overnight hack on top of a job. Keep it to the hours they have already blocked, over a few days, with a hard stop.

**Students.** A real task from a role Masai actually places into. The output should look like the work a hiring partner can recognise: a working piece, not a slide about a startup.

**Experienced.** A harder real problem, where the output is something they can defend to a technical outsider: an architecture, a tradeoff, a thing that runs. This is the raw material for the pitch. It is not a second placement test.

The problem is released when the window starts. Everyone in a track gets the same problem, so the solutions can be compared. Each person gets a different twist only at the defense, so a shared solution does not transfer.

### How “level of problem solved” is scored

“Level” is not how fancy the deck is. Four checks, same for both tracks. Reviewers do not see years of experience while scoring.

| Check | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| It runs | A stranger can open it. A write-up with no build does not pass. |
| It answers the problem that was set | Scope they invented to look bigger does not count. |
| They can change it live | The judge adds one requirement on the call. The person edits the work while the screen is shared. This is the plagiarism filter. A copied file stalls here. |
| They can say who it is for, in a few minutes | Students: would a hiring manager trust this. Experienced: would a technical person fund another week of it. |

Take a hard cap, on the order of 12 per track. A cap is a room. A “top 1%” label is a poster.

People who never show up to the live defense are out, even if the file is perfect.

## What each track gets

Say this in the invite, before they start. If the room on the other side is not booked, do not open that track.

### Students: a job path, not a job guarantee

The perk is one booked interview, or a partner review of this hackathon build, for everyone who makes the cap. The partner has already agreed to take those conversations.

That is stronger than a normal batch referral, because the partner is looking at a piece of work Masai just watched them build. It is weaker than a promise of a placement, on purpose. One missed guarantee ends the club.

### Experienced: network they can use, and a public proof

The perk is a technical pitching round and a summit. The names are on the invite: the mentor, the operators, the founders. They sit for the pitches. They are not a recording.

What this is for:

- A mentor who has seen the work and will take a follow-up
- A room that can turn into referrals
- A talk or a demo the person can point to at work, which is the promotion path

Investment is not part of the offer. If an angel or a fund agrees to sit in that room for a given cohort, say so for that cohort only, and only after the date is fixed. Scaler told fellows an investment door might open. Sixteen months later no cheque was public. Do not put “may be investment” on the Masai version until the cheque-writer is named.

## What not to import

| From | Leave it out |
| --- | --- |
| Scaler Luminaries | A badge, dinners, and an open door to funding with no cheque |
| Course evaluations | Using existing online scores to pick the room. Those files are easy to copy |
| Thiel / Campus Fund | Asking part-time students to quit work, or to already own 25% of a company |
| A placement guarantee | Language Masai has already ruled out on the placement pledge |

What is worth copying from the programs that lasted: a small room, a short clock, a perk that is money or a meeting already booked, and a filter that is the work itself.

## First run

1. Pick one live part-time batch. Split it in advance into the two tracks by what they want (job, or network), not by guessing who is “smart.”
2. Book the student-side interviews and the experienced-side pitch room before announcing the hackathon.
3. Replace the normal sessions for that short window. One problem per track. Live defense is mandatory.
4. Cut to about 12 and 12 on the live-change score.
5. Deliver the interview and the pitch in the following week.
6. Publish four numbers: how many started, how many defended live, how many got the interview or the pitch, how many of those moved (offer, intro, or a second meeting).

If those numbers are missing, the second cohort is a story. That is where Luminaries ended up.
