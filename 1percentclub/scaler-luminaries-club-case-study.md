# Case study: Scaler Luminaries Club



The company is **Scaler** (InterviewBit Technologies), Scaler School of Technology (SST), Electronic City, Bengaluru. People often write it as Scalar. This is not Scalar Capital and not Scalar Academy (Bandung).

## Verdict

**The club has not succeeded.**

Sixteen months after launch (7 June 2025 to 5 October 2026), the public record shows a club that still meets, and no company, cheque, or revenue that came out of it. The 23 were full-time students in SST’s residential undergraduate program, already enrolled on the 4-year pathway (parallel BSc alongside the campus program), not an outside or part-time cohort.

Scaler set its own bar on launch day: weekly mentorship with Prasanna Sankar, and a path from those rooms to building real companies. What followed in public is two events. Orientation day, then a dinner in January 2026 where six students pitched and were told a later investment conversation was possible if the case was strong. No round was announced after that. No second cohort has been published. Weekly sessions were promised and never shown.

The strongest member outcome is MetaCognition (Krish Jaiswal, Priyam Ghosh, Sauhard Gupta): papers, conference invites, pilot conversations, and a pitch at Scaler’s own investor summit by August 2026. They have not published a raise or revenue. The other fellows have no public company result tied to the club.

The program did produce a marketing asset. It sits on the SST admissions page as Scaler’s boldest program, and Prasanna’s tuition scholarship was announced with the club as the proof point. That helps SST sell seats. It is a separate result from the club working for the 23 students in it.

These students are still in college, so a funded company was never a fair sixteen-month test of them. It is a fair test of the program. A club whose pitch is access to a unicorn founder and to capital has, so far, delivered dinners.

## What the club is

An invite-only founder circle inside SST. Scaler describes the 23 members as learning to think like the world’s top 1%. The operating format is pitch critique, first-principles drills, and dinners with Prasanna Sankar (Rippling cofounder, founder of 0xPPL).

Prasanna’s line, as reported from the sessions: this is about finding the few who will change the game, not about raising the average.

What members were told they would get:

- Direct mentorship from Prasanna, plus Anshuman Singh and Abhimanyu Saxena from inauguration day
- Weekly sessions with Prasanna and other industry leaders (stated on 7 June 2025, not independently documented after that)
- Real-time pitch critique and prototyping inside the Scaler Innovation Lab
- In some cases, an open door to a later pitch and investment discussion

What was never part of the published design: a grant, a standard cheque, or equity terms for members.

## Who the students were

The 23 were full-time students already enrolled at SST. They lived on campus in a residential undergraduate program. This was not an open fellowship for working professionals, dropouts, or applicants from other colleges.

| Fact | What SST and the members published |
| --- | --- |
| Status | Full-time students, already inside the school. The club is an invite cut of enrolled students. |
| Level | Undergraduate. A 4-year program taken after Grade 12. Entry rule on the AI & Business page: age 20 or under as of 1 July of the intake year. |
| Where they were in the program | Mid-course, not new admits. Sai Vishwas Gooty’s split was 9 seniors and 14 from his own batch. MetaCognition’s three founders were described as third-year students in 2026. Priyam Ghosh described himself as 19 at onboarding in June 2025. |
| Campus | Fully residential, Electronic City, Bengaluru. Hostel and mess run for the first three years. Students are on campus while they attend the club. |

IIT Madras BS is the other parallel option named on the admissions page. |

They were in class, in a hostel, on a four-year undergraduate clock. The club sat on top of that enrolment. It did not replace it, and it did not pay them to leave it.

“Top 1%” is Scaler’s long-running brand line, reused here as a mindset. It is not a scored national rank. The only selection math on record is inside SST.

One selected student, Sai Vishwas Gooty, published the split (June 2025):

| Gate | Selected | Pool they published | Rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Luminaries, seniors | 9 | 180+ | 5.0% |
| Luminaries, his batch | 14 | 430+ | 3.3% |
| Luminaries, both pools | 23 | 610+ | 3.8% |

SST’s own 2025 admissions page (AI & Business) prints two figures for the same intake that do not match:

| Gate | Selected | Pool | Rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Headline selection rate | 250 | not stated | 3.3% |
| Funnel on the same page | 250 selected, 1,000 interviewed | 15,000 NSET takers | 1.7% (250 / 15,000) |

Luminaries is a cut inside an already selected campus. It is about 4% of the student pools one member cited, not 1% of India.

## Member outcome that is actually visible: MetaCognition

Krish Jaiswal, Priyam Ghosh, and Sauhard Gupta. Started inside an SST college club as a prototype called Memora (could beginners learn from research papers), then rebuilt as a neuroscience-inspired memory layer for AI agents.

By late August 2026, their own and Scaler’s posts claim:

- 3 preprints
- Presented at IEEE ICACI in China, invited to a Rome neuroscience conference
- Advice or validation conversations with researchers at UC Berkeley, MIT Neuroscience, and (in Priyam’s 26 Aug 2026 post) Google DeepMind and OpenAI
- Pilots with multiple companies over about seven months of research, products described as close to public launch
- A pitch at SAIS 2026 that “picked up investor attention”

No round size. No revenue figure. A $10M pre-seed reported in September 2026 belongs to a different, Australian company also named Metacognition (Main Sequence). Do not merge those two.

## What other organisations do

Four programs are the public versions of “pick a few young builders, put operators in the room, attach capital.” None of them is an edtech company. None of them has a full-time or residential student body. They do not select in-house students, because they have no batch. People apply from outside. Course marks are not the screen.

### How they filter, since they cannot use their own exams

They look for someone who has already built something they can inspect. A copied assignment never enters the process.

**<a href="https://www.antler.co/location/india" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Antler India Fellowship</a>.** Open application, 2021. About 2,400 students from 700 colleges and 375 cities applied. Antler kept 8 teams, 19 people, who were already trying an idea on the side of college: blood tests, cattle feed, ticketing, and others. Mentors included founders from Quizizz, Headout, and Little Black Book. The grant was $20,000, equity-free. After working with the cohort, Antler said it invested in 6 of those startups. Cattle Guru (cattle-feed delivery, from the team first called Tezz) is one they later funded. Acceptance was about 0.3% of applications. The filter was the application plus work already started, then months of watching the team, then a cheque for some of them. Antler’s headline program now is the India AI Residency: ₹4 crore for 11% equity, a ₹2 lakh expense grant, decision in about 3 weeks, two weeks in Bengaluru. That residency is for founders generally, not a student batch.

**<a href="https://www.zfellows.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Z Fellows</a>.** About 10 people in a cohort. You apply with a side project you are already making. A reviewer looks at that work. The ones taken spend one week with founders (the room has included founders from Figma, Instacart, and similar companies). The money is $10,000, optional, as an investment at a very high valuation cap. A person with a full-time job can do the week and go back to the job. There is no class rank. It is still running cohorts years later. The length is one week, so the program does not depend on an ongoing club.

**<a href="https://thielfellowship.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Thiel Fellowship</a>** (<a href="https://www.thielfoundation.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Thiel Foundation</a>). $250,000 over two years, no equity, plus introductions to investors. You must be 22 or under and skip or quit college. You apply with the work you will do instead of the degree. They interview. Dylan Field (Figma) and Ritesh Agarwal (OYO) came through it. Leaving college is the filter. Someone who only looks strong on a take-home assignment does not give up the degree to enter.

**<a href="https://yourcampusfund.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Campus Fund</a>.** It will not take someone for being a good student. The person must already own at least 25% of a real company, and must be a current student, a dropout, or a graduate from the last three years. They pitch that company. Fund III is $100 million, with first cheques of ₹1–8 crore, aimed at 60–80 companies, about half the fund reserved for follow-on. The company is the proof.

## What held up, and what is still a claim

Solid, because more than one written source agrees, or the primary post is specific:

- A 23-person invite club launched 7 June 2025
- Prasanna Sankar was in the room, in person
- At least one later dinner of six pitches, with no announced investment
- Scholarship slabs
- ₹10 lakh NeoSapien grant
- Selection split of 9 seniors and 14 from one batch

Soft, because it appears on a Scaler marketing page or in a single press cycle:

- Weekly mentorship after launch day
- The ₹2 crore student seed pool
- Samsung’s ₹25 lakh
- Revenue for Ather Labs and Trackaroo
- “Investor attention” with no amount

## What to copy, and what failed

Useful design, independent of the outcome:

- A hard cap (23), so the room stays small enough for a real operator to sit at the table
- The mentor is a founder who still builds, not a visiting lecturer
- The session is a pitch with pushback, not a keynote
- Dinner, no stage

What failed against the promise:

- No capital product for the people in the room. The scholarship pays future students’ fees. The Innovation Lab writes grants to outside startups.
- Cadence was announced and then disappeared from the public record. Two events in sixteen months is a brand moment, not a fellowship.
- Success metrics were never published: no list of companies, no revenue, no cheques, no second cohort.
- “Top 1%” does recruiting work. Inside the building the cut is about 4% of SST.
