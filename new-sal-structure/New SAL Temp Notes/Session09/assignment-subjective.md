# Assignment Subjective

## Task

Answer Question 1 through Question 9 in short, direct writing. Use only the facts written inside each question.

Question 1. In two sentences, state what traditional software does, and what happens when the same allowed input is used again.

Question 2. In two sentences, state what generative AI produces. From this list, name the one job that is generative: a spam label, a new polite rewrite of a sentence you supply, a turnstile that opens only for a stored ticket code.

Question 3. In two sentences, state what a large language model does with the text you type. Also state one of these that it is not: a person who knows you, a live copy of every website, or a diary of files you did not provide.

Question 4. In two sentences, explain next-word prediction. The text so far is `Please submit the`. Give one sensible next piece and one unlikely next piece.

Question 5. Define a token in one sentence. Split `The shop is open.` into small pieces. Include the full stop as its own piece.

Question 6. Define a prompt in one sentence. Rewrite this weak prompt so the job, the reader, and a short length are clear: `Tell me about databases.` Do not add fake facts.

Question 7. Define a hallucination in one sentence. The only given facts are: the student is Kabir, Kabir studies in Pune, and Kabir wants leave on Monday because of fever. A reply also claims a 2022 state gold medal, attendance of 99.4 percent, and that the principal already approved the leave. List those three added claims. Then write a two-line leave message that uses only the given facts.

Question 8. Name two jobs these tools are good at, and two jobs they are weak at. For each weak job, give one reason.

Question 9. A reply is grammatically correct and adds a bus fare that was not in the prompt and not on a notice you can open. State what you delete, what kind of wording you may keep, and why correct grammar is not proof of the fare.

### What a complete answer includes

- Question 4 names a likely piece such as `form` or `assignment`, and an unlikely piece such as `river`.
- Question 5 shows pieces along the lines of `The`, `shop`, `is`, `open`, and `.`.
- Question 6 names a beginner reader and a short length, such as four lines.
- Question 7's leave message can mention Kabir, Pune, Monday, and fever. It does not mention the medal, 99.4 percent, or an approval.

### Constraints

- Write in sentences or short lines.
- Do not write Python or SQL.
- Do not treat a smooth sentence as a checked fact.
- Do not add awards, percentages, fares, or approvals that the question did not give you.

### Submission Instruction

- Type the answer in the answer box.

## Answer Explanation

Question 1. Traditional software carries out rules a person wrote, so each allowed input maps to a result those rules already define. Using the same allowed input again usually gives that same planned result. A calculator that returns 25 for 5 times 5 is an example, because the multiply rule was programmed.

Question 2. Generative AI produces new content, such as sentences, summaries, or lists, instead of only choosing a label. The generative job in the list is the new polite rewrite of a sentence you supply. The spam label is a classifier, and the turnstile is a match against stored data.

Question 3. A large language model continues and reshapes language from the text you type. It is not a diary of files you did not provide. It is also not a person who knows you, and it is not a live copy of every website.

Question 4. Next-word prediction chooses a probable next piece for the text so far, then another piece, until a reply is long enough. For `Please submit the`, a sensible next piece is `form` or `assignment`. `river` is not a sensible continuation.

Question 5. A token is a small piece of text, often a word, part of a word, a number, or a mark. One simple split of the sentence is `The` / `shop` / `is` / `open` / `.`.

Question 6. A prompt is the text you submit to start or steer the reply. A clearer prompt is: "Explain what a database is in four short lines for a beginner who has not studied computers." Another acceptable version can ask for five lines instead of four, as long as the reader and the length are named and no fake facts are added.

Question 7. A hallucination is a claim written as if it were factual when it is invented or not supported by a source you can check. The three added claims are the 2022 state gold medal, attendance of 99.4 percent, and the principal's approval. A safe two-line message is: "I, Kabir, request leave on Monday because I have fever. I study in Pune and will complete pending work after I return."

Question 8. Strong jobs include rewriting a rough sentence whose meaning is already yours, and summarising text you paste into the prompt. Weak jobs include inventing a citation, because the paper may not exist, and stating today's bus fare, because the model may not have that live fact. Explaining a term in simple words is another strong job if you compare it with notes. Claiming the contents of a private file you did not provide is another weak job.

Question 9. Delete the bus fare, because it was not in the prompt and you cannot open a notice for it. Keep wording that still does the job you asked for and does not depend on that number. Correct grammar only shows that the sentence is well formed. It does not show that the fare is true.

One alternative for Question 6 is to ask for a five-line explanation for a beginner instead of four lines. The required parts stay the same: the job, the reader, and a short length, with no invented facts.
