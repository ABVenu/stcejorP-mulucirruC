# Initial Findings

Survey of degree programmes in drones, aerospace, robotics, and physical AI, for an online bachelor’s in artificial intelligence, robotics, and autonomous intelligence. An aircraft is one machine inside that degree.



Each programme was checked against four conditions.

1. It awards a full bachelor’s, with a way to exit earlier.
2. It can be completed online, without a workshop, a personal drone, or a flight line.
3. Machine learning, deep learning, and robot learning are core, not elective.
4. Robotics and autonomous intelligence are the machine subject. An aircraft may be one system inside that subject.

No programme in this survey meets all four.

---

## Summary

Online degrees exist for drone operations. Embry-Riddle’s bachelor’s is the strongest of those, and it does not teach a machine-learning sequence. Online degrees exist for robotics. IU’s distance B.Eng. is the strongest of those. Artificial intelligence is an elective there, and there is no aircraft. Campus degrees combine robotics, artificial intelligence, and drones. IILM, UPES, IPSA, Efrei, and Beihang are in that group, and they assume a laboratory.

The nearest Indian neighbour is the IIT Madras B.S. in Aeronautics and Space Technology: a non-campus aerospace bachelor’s, with one machine-learning course, a UAV design project, and compulsory labs on the IIT Madras campus. Physical AI became a bachelor’s title in 2026 at TH Augsburg, on campus, in German, with a robotics laboratory in the early semesters and no aerospace spine.

The open slot is an online bachelor’s whose core is intelligence and robotics. An aircraft is one machine that intelligence operates, not the subject of the degree.

### Findings

IIT Madras aeronautics is the programme to stand beside. Copying its CFD, structures, and propulsion core would make a thinner aerospace degree, and those courses want the campus labs IIT Madras already requires. IIT Madras analyses the vehicle. This degree builds the software that perceives, decides, and commands a robot, and an aircraft as one of those machines.

IIT Guwahati shows that an IIT will teach robotics and artificial intelligence with recorded classes and a large project. It is a master’s, and it assumes a prior engineering degree. A graduate of the proposed bachelor’s should be able to enter a programme of that kind.

IIT Delhi is evidence of demand for a short robotics certificate. A five-month survey does not replace a degree. Its capstone, build one robot, assumes hardware.

Fees are public for the IIT Madras aeronautics B.S.: about ₹6.12 lakh across all levels before waivers, paid per course. That figure is a reference for an IIT non-campus bachelor’s. It is not a fee for this proposal.

### Findings

Embry-Riddle is the precedent for teaching uncrewed systems without living on an airfield: sensing, mission planning, regulation, and aerodynamics at the level of an operator and analyst. Its worldwide course list is a source for aerial-systems and regulation content. It is not a source for the learning and control sequence.

The certificates at Purdue, Wyoming, and Sonoran confirm a jobs market in flying and data products. That market is served by short credentials. A four-year degree that only repeats them has no academic reason to exist.

Utah State is a warning about the phrase “online robotics degree.” The robotics content is transferred in from a workshop. The online part is management.

## Physical AI

Physical AI, as NVIDIA uses the term, is an artificial intelligence system that perceives, reasons, and acts in the physical world. A model that only writes text is outside that definition. A model that turns camera images and a task into motor commands is inside it. Universities launching degrees in 2026 use that English title or the academic synonym, embodied intelligence.

NVIDIA’s short courses are simulation-first: a digital twin, synthetic data, a policy trained by reinforcement learning or imitation, a vision-language-action model, then an optional move onto a physical robot. That stack can be taught without a laboratory. The courses last hours or days. They are not a degree.

### Findings

TH Augsburg is the bachelor’s reference for the name Physical AI: learning, vision, control, and simulation on a robot. QAIRU states the scope that an online degree can keep: hardware only as far as the software needs it. The later layer, a vision-language-action model, synthetic data, and learning from demonstration, belongs after vision, control, and reinforcement learning. It does not require a robot in the learner’s house.

Beihang joins aerospace to embodied intelligence on a campus, with laboratories. An aircraft in this degree is one machine inside the robotics courses, not a second department.

### Findings

IPSA’s master’s is the reference for later work on an aircraft: sensor fusion, visual tracking, urban mission planning, and deep learning, graded as projects. Efrei is the reference for ROS, navigation, and fleet control in the same major as the drone.

IPSA’s bachelor’s splits drones and artificial intelligence in year two. This degree keeps both, with robotics and intelligence as the priority.

The French regulator publishes pilot theory online and keeps flying hours outside the engineering degree. Airspace rules can sit in a core course. DGCA flight hours stay with a training organisation, outside the credit total.

HADA is certification, airframe architecture, and payloads. Those want a design office and a hangar.

UWS does not teach the four-year sequence underneath: mathematics, a vehicle model, control, machine learning, and vision. Its entry rule assumes the first degree did.

## Conclusions

**IIT Madras aeronautics.** The non-campus shape is real inside the IIT system: recorded courses, in-person exams, a qualifier, and stackable exits. The subject is not the one to copy. Their core is fluids, structures, CFD, and propulsion. One machine-learning course does not make it a physical-AI degree.

**IIT Guwahati.** Robotics, vision, and machine learning can be the named core of an IIT award, with a large project. That is a master’s. The bachelor’s should make a graduate eligible to enter it.

**Embry-Riddle.** Online learners can study sensing, mission planning, aerodynamics at operator depth, and airspace rules. That content belongs in the courses where an aircraft is the example, not in place of the learning sequence.

**IU.** A robotics bachelor’s can be assessed through modelling and simulation, with no kit posted to the learner. The learning sequence here is compulsory. At IU it is elective.

**TH Augsburg and QAIRU.** Physical AI, as a bachelor’s title in 2026, means a robot plus machine learning, fusion, control, vision, and simulation. The study plan is [TH Augsburg, B.Sc. Physical AI](https://www.tha.de/en/university/faculties/computer-science/study-program/ki-und-robotik-bsc/). QAIRU states the scope: hardware only as far as the software needs it.

**NVIDIA.** The late undergraduate layer is a vision-language-action model and synthetic data, trained and scored in simulation, after reinforcement learning and vision. [What is Physical AI](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/generative-physical-ai/). [Physical AI learning path](https://docs.nvidia.com/learning/physical-ai/index.html).

**IPSA’s master’s and Efrei.** Sensor fusion, visual tracking, mission planning, ROS, and fleet behaviour are the later robotics subjects worth staffing. IPSA’s bachelor’s split, drones or artificial intelligence, is the choice this degree does not make.

**UWS.** A capstone mission can be inspection, agriculture, logistics, or environmental monitoring, graded by demonstration. The pilot certificate stays out of the credit total.

**Regulators.** Airspace theory can be taught in the degree. The hours that produce a remote-pilot licence belong to a training organisation.
